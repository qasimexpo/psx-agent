## 1 · Book closures: same 120%, different yield — `events-2026-10-01.jpg`

**GAL and GHNI both declared a 120% dividend. One pays you twice the yield of the other. And the last day to qualify for either is tomorrow.**

Ghandhara Automobiles (GAL) and Ghandhara Industries (GHNI) both declared a 120% final dividend, and both books close on Tuesday 6 October.

First, the deadline. PSX settles two business days after the trade, and there is a weekend in between. A purchase on Friday 2 October settles on Tuesday the 6th and counts. One on Monday the 5th settles on Wednesday — too late. So today and tomorrow are the only two sessions left.

Now the part the headline number hides. A PSX dividend percentage is measured on the Rs 10 par value, never on the share price. So 120% is Rs 12 a share for both companies — identical — but they trade at very different prices:

GAL — Rs 543.95 — Rs 12 is a 2.2% yield
GHNI — Rs 1,068.00 — Rs 12 is a 1.1% yield

Same announcement, same rupees, and one pays twice the yield of the other. "120%" tells you nothing about which is the better payout until you divide by the price.

After 15% withholding tax for filers, each share pays Rs 10.20. Non-filers lose 30%, leaving Rs 8.40.

One more honest point, because this is where people get caught: a dividend is not free money for buying the day before. On the ex-date the price typically falls by about the dividend, since that cash is leaving the company. Buy only if you would want to own the share anyway.

Both are Shariah compliant on the KMI All Shares Islamic Index, and both sit in the KSE-100 and the KMI-30. Both have also fallen about 17–18% over the past month.

Further out on the card: GOC (20%, last buying day Monday 5 October) and NCPL (10%, Wednesday 7 October). AGMs: TPLI's EOGM tomorrow, PRL on Saturday, ISL on Monday.

## 5 · Why LUCK is now LUCKXD — `suffixes-2026-10-01.jpg`

**Can't find LUCK in your broker's app today? It's there — as LUCKXD. Twenty PSX companies have grown a suffix, twelve of them in the KSE-100.**

When a company's book closes for a dividend, the exchange marks its ticker for a while: LUCK becomes LUCKXD, INDU becomes INDUXD. It is the same company and the same share. The suffix tells you one thing: the payout has already been claimed by whoever was on the register. Buy LUCKXD today and you get the share, not the dividend that was just declared.

That is also why the price steps down around the ex-date — the cash is on its way out of the company, so it comes out of the share price too.

Today's list includes some of the biggest names on the exchange: Lucky Cement, Indus Motor, Colgate-Palmolive, International Steels, Fauji Cement — and the Pakistan Stock Exchange itself, trading as PSXXD ahead of its AGM on Tuesday.

The three suffixes worth knowing:
XD — ex-dividend
XB — ex-bonus: the bonus shares stay with the old holders
XR — ex-rights: the right to buy new shares stays with the old holders

## 6 · Stopped at the limit — `limits-2026-10-01.jpg`

**Five PSX shares hit the exchange's 10% limit by lunchtime. Two of today's names were on this list a week ago, too.**

As of about 12:50 PKT:

DSIL — Rs 15.70 — **+10.02%**
ASHT — Rs 45.51 — **−10.01%**
MWMP — Rs 84.78 — **+10.00%**
FIMM — Rs 286.94 — **+10.00%**
FZCM — Rs 280.46 — **+10.00%**

Also at the cap, just off the card: DFML (+10.00%) and FPJM (−9.99%).

PSX limits how far a share can move in one session. At the limit, orders past that price simply queue — a "limit up" share can be close to unbuyable for the rest of the day, a "limit down" one close to unsellable.

The repeat names are the interesting part. DSIL hit the upper limit on 24 September at Rs 10.58; five sessions later it is at Rs 15.70, up about 48%. FPJM was at the lower limit that day as well and is there again today. Shares that keep arriving at the limit are usually small, cheap and thinly traded — exactly where a little money moves the price a long way, in both directions.

FIMM, for what it is worth, has a 100% final dividend with its book closing on 14 October.

A limit is a trading rule, not a verdict on the company. Educational only, not financial advice.
