## 1 · Dividend calculator, worked on JVDC — `feature-dividend-JVDC.jpg`

**JVDC declared a 60% final dividend. That is Rs 6 a share — not 60% of the price.**

This is the most common misreading of a PSX announcement, so here it is worked all the way through.

A percentage dividend is quoted on the **Rs 10 par value**, never on the market price. So 60% is Rs 6.00 per share. Against JVDC's current Rs 146.61 that is a 4.1% gross yield — on this one payout, not for the year.

On 400 shares:

400 × Rs 6.00 = Rs 2,400 gross
Withholding tax, filer at 15% = − Rs 360
Paid into your account = **Rs 2,040**

If you are not on the Active Taxpayers List the rate is 30%, so the same payout nets Rs 1,680 instead. Being on the ATL is worth Rs 360 on this one dividend alone.

Javedan Corporation is Shariah compliant on the KMI All Shares Islamic Index and a KSE-100 constituent. Its book closes on Wednesday 30 September — and because PSX settles two business days after the trade, the last session that still counts is Monday 28 September.

**Four PSX payouts close their books on 30 September. Because settlement is T+2, the last day that counts is Monday the 28th — three sessions from now, including today.**

Closing on Wednesday 30 September:

JVDC — Javedan Corporation — 60% final dividend, Rs 6.00 a share
JKSM — J.K. Spinning Mills — 20% final dividend, Rs 2.00 a share
JVDCPS — Javedan Corporation (Pref) — 12% final dividend
TCORPCPS — Tariq Corporation (Pref) — 10% final dividend

Here is the part that catches people out every month. A PSX trade settles two business days later, and you have to be on the register when the book closes. A trade on Monday the 28th settles on Wednesday the 30th and counts. A trade on Tuesday the 29th does not, however early in the morning you place it. That leaves Thursday, Friday and Monday. If an exchange holiday lands in that window, leave yourself another day.

Yesterday's post on ARPL is the live example of getting this wrong: its 450% interim dividend closed the book today, which means the window had already shut back on the 22nd. A declared dividend and an available dividend are not the same thing.

## 3 · COLG, on AGM day — `stock-COLG.jpg`

**Colgate-Palmolive holds its AGM at 3pm today. If you own the shares, that is a meeting you are entitled to attend.**

Most retail holders on the PSX never use this. An Annual General Meeting is where shareholders vote — on the accounts, on the final dividend, on re-appointing the auditors and the directors. You are entitled to attend, ask questions and vote your shares, or to appoint a proxy to do it for you. Companies now run these as hybrid meetings, so attendance is usually a video link rather than a trip to Karachi.

## 4 · INDU — the familiar name that fails the screen — `stock-INDU.jpg`

**Indus Motor assembles Toyotas in Pakistan, sits in the KSE-100, and is not Shariah compliant. Big and familiar is not the same as compliant.**

This is the single most common assumption we see, and INDU is the cleanest example of it being wrong. It is one of the most recognisable companies on the exchange and a KSE-100 constituent — and it is not in the KMI All Shares Islamic Index, the exchange's own Shariah-screened list. Index membership is decided by that screen, not by how well you know the brand.

The page states it in the first line either way, for all 547 listed companies. We are not issuing a ruling; we are reporting the exchange's.

The rest of what it shows, at Rs 1,841.00 (+0.99% today): down 20.6% over a year and below its 20-day, 50-day and 200-day averages, with RSI at 26.8.

One part of that decline is worth separating out, because it is arithmetic rather than sentiment. INDU paid a **470% final dividend — Rs 47 a share** — and the book closed on 22 September. When a dividend comes out of a company, it comes out of the share price too: Rs 47 on a price near Rs 1,870 is about 2.5%. A chunk of the recent drop is the payout leaving, not the market changing its mind. This is also why you sometimes see a ticker written INDUXD on the board — XD for ex-dividend, the flag that the payout has detached from the price.

The AGM is on 28 September.

## 5 · Who hit the daily cap — `limits-2026-09-24.jpg`

**Five PSX shares stopped dead within a hundredth of a point of each other today. That is not a coincidence — it is the exchange's price cap.**

Today's list:

DSIL — Rs 10.58 — **+10.44%**
FCIBL — Rs 37.17 — **+10.00%**
PKGI — Rs 37.13 — **−10.01%**
FPJM — Rs 29.63 — **−9.99%**
SGPL — Rs 28.57 — **−9.99%**

PSX limits how far a share may move in a single session. Once a share reaches the cap it cannot print beyond it, so orders past that point simply queue — which is why a "limit up" share can look unbuyable and a "limit down" one unsellable for the rest of the day. The exchange publishes the list of capped shares on its own data portal.

The cap is set as a percentage of the previous close **or** a flat rupee amount, whichever is larger. You can watch the rupee part doing the work in today's data: DSIL moved exactly **Rs 1.00**, and on a Rs 9.58 previous close that is 10.44% — which is why it shows a bigger percentage than everything else on the list. The cheaper the share, the further it can travel in percentage terms.

Two things worth taking from this.

First, look at the prices. Every name on that list trades between Rs 10 and Rs 37, and not one of them is in the KSE-100. Daily percentage leaderboards are dominated by the market's smallest and cheapest shares, because a small rupee move is a large percentage of a small price. A 10% day on a Rs 10 share is one rupee.

Second, FPJM and SGPL are both Shariah compliant, and both fell to the cap today. Compliance is a screen for what a business does and how it is financed. It is not a rating of quality, safety or valuation, and it never predicts a session.
